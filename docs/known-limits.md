# Known limits (a list for the README)

Kept here so the README's "failures and known limits" section can be written from it. Each item says what happens, not what is planned. Last updated 2026-10-15 (tester round 1).

1. **Two-part questions answer only one part, silently.** A question that asks two different things ("when is X and how do I do Y") gets an answer to one of them and no sign that the other was dropped. (Questions about two courses are handled: up to three course codes are answered together.)
2. **A rate-limited request returns HTTP 200 by design.** The reply is a normal assistant message ("You're asking quickly. Please wait a moment and try again.") with `answer_type: "guardrail"`; the reason is in the log row, in `/health` and in `how_found.route_detail`. Likewise the daily-cap, kill-switch and timeout replies.
3. **"How this was found" shows the confidence cutoff and the cost on purpose.** The panel is meant to be inspected: the best match's similarity, the cutoff, the sources and the cost per step are shown to every visitor.
4. **Non-English handling is being decided separately.** Nothing is claimed about it here.
5. **Noisy questions can still fall under the confidence cutoff.** Markup, emoji and redaction placeholders are removed from the search text and the classifier input, but a short question can still score just under the cutoff of 0.59 and be answered with "I could not find this" (tester round 1: four such questions stayed under it after cleaning; the cutoff was not lowered).
6. **The tuition due dates come out as one run-on sentence** (the Bursar payment table is read by the model). An optional code-written route needs owner-verified rows and is deferred.
7. **Advice answers are long.** A "should I drop or withdraw" answer holds the rules, the dates and a calendar list; the list collapses to five items on the page, but the text still repeats some dates.
8. **Courses outside the four departments are not covered.** The assistant says so and points to the catalog; a course named inside a prerequisite text but absent from the data is marked "(no catalog record)".
9. **Distress and stress detection is plain phrase matching.** It can miss a worried student who uses other words and can catch an ordinary sentence that happens to use one of the phrases; the replies are fixed and kind, and the student can ask again.
10. **The rate limit counts per network address in memory.** Students behind one shared address (a campus gateway, or a proxy that hides the visitor) share one count; the counts reset when the service restarts.
11. **A question in another language gets an English-only notice (option 3).** The assistant detects the language with one small model call, runs its safety screens on the English translation (so a non-English distress or injection message is still caught), and then replies, in English, that it currently answers in English only and asks the student to ask again in English; a one-line note in the student's language comes first. It does not answer non-English questions. Option 1 (answer in English from the translation) is built behind `guardrails.non_english: translate` but failed its pre-registered probe rule (see `docs/decisions.md`).
12. **The translations used to test this are model-made and the owner could not verify them.** The probes (`docs/language-probes.jsonl`) check retrieval, route and dates only, not translation quality; the note line in the student's language comes from the model and is checked only for shape (short, no digits, no links).
13. **The language step adds one small model call, only on questions that do not look English** (about $0.0001 to $0.0005 each); English-looking questions skip it. A short English question with few common English words (for example "Last day to withdraw?") can reach the step; it is detected as English and changes nothing.
14. **Language detection can fail.** If the call fails or its reply is unusable, the question goes through as it is today (the original text, no note); a non-English question can then abstain as it did before this round.
15. **The cutoff stays 0.59 because an out-of-scope question and a valid short question can score within 0.01 of each other** (q45 "What days and times does MAT 1475 meet this fall?" at 0.568 against the tester's plain "whats the tuition deadline" at 0.576), so no cutoff separates them. Instead a **near-miss reply** lists the closest page (and a second one only if its own similarity is also within 0.04 of the cutoff) when a below-threshold abstention is within 0.04 of the cutoff; it makes no claim about what those pages say, and it is still an abstention. The first page is the pipeline's first, in the order the reranker gives (an LLM call, so the order can differ between runs), even if it is not the page with the highest similarity.
16. **Questions below the near-miss band abstain with the old wording** and no pages. The tester's advisor question ("do i need an appointment to see my advisor or can i just walk in") scored 0.549, just under the band (0.55 to 0.59), so it gets the old sentence even though the advisement page exists.
17. **q98 stays incorrect:** "my phone is [PHONE], when is my tuition bill due this fall?" scores just under the cutoff after cleaning, so it gets a near-miss reply (the Bill Payment Deadlines page) instead of the answer; a near-miss reply is still an abstention, so the judge calls it an abstention error.
18. **A question about the student's own aid or bill that uses none of the listed account signals is no longer refused as a personal-account question.** A possessive plus an office noun ("my financial aid", "my tuition") now needs a word such as status, approved, denied, deposited, owe, "did my" or "why is my"; a question like "is my aid ok" that abstains gets the plain abstention wording with the office's contact card, not the personal-account message.
19. **Optional LangSmith tracing is a developer tool and is off by default.** When the owner turns it on (config flag, `CTA_TRACING=on` and a key), the redacted question text and the assistant's reply of every served request go to LangSmith, a third party, and stay there for the retention period of the account (the documentation lists 14 days for base retention; the owner confirms the account setting). Requests blocked by a guardrail are not traced. A name that does not follow "my name is" is not removed from the text before it is sent. Turning it on for a public deployment is a separate owner decision.
