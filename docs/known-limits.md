# Known limits (a list for the README)

Kept here so the README's "failures and known limits" section can be written from it. Each item says what happens, not what is planned. Last updated 2026-10-16, after the casual round (items 1 to 30 cover tester rounds 1, 1b and 2 and the casual round); items 31 to 34 were added for the final milestone.

1. **Two-part questions answer only one part, silently.** A question that asks two different things ("when is X and how do I do Y") gets an answer to one of them and no sign that the other was dropped. (Questions about two courses are handled: up to three course codes are answered together.)
2. **A rate-limited request returns HTTP 200 by design.** The reply is a normal assistant message ("You're asking quickly. Please wait a moment and try again.") with `answer_type: "guardrail"`; the reason is in the log row, in `/health` and in `how_found.route_detail`. Likewise the daily-cap, kill-switch and timeout replies.
3. **"How this was found" shows the confidence cutoff and the cost on purpose, but only on request.** The panel is hidden by default and appears when the page address has `?details=1` (final milestone, part 6); sources and citations always stay visible. When shown, it is meant to be inspected: the best match's similarity, the cutoff, the sources and the cost per step are shown to every visitor.
4. **Non-English questions are not answered (decided: option 3, English-only notice).** The assistant replies in English that it answers in English only; see item 11 for how it works and for the option 1 probe that failed.
5. **Noisy questions can still fall under the confidence cutoff.** Markup, emoji and redaction placeholders are removed from the search text and the classifier input, but a short question can still score just under the cutoff of 0.59 and be answered with "I could not find this" (tester round 1: four such questions stayed under it after cleaning; the cutoff was not lowered).
6. **The tuition due dates come out as one run-on sentence** (the Bursar payment table is read by the model). An optional code-written route needs owner-verified rows and is deferred.
7. **Advice answers are long.** A "should I drop or withdraw" answer holds the rules, the dates and a calendar list; the list collapses to five items on the page, but the text still repeats some dates.
8. **Courses outside the four departments are not covered.** The assistant says so and points to the catalog; a course named inside a prerequisite text but absent from the data is marked "(no catalog record)".
9. **Distress and stress detection is plain phrase matching.** It can miss a worried student who uses other words and can catch an ordinary sentence that happens to use one of the phrases; the replies are fixed and kind, and the student can ask again.
10. **The rate limit counts per network address in memory.** Students behind one shared address (a campus gateway, or a proxy that hides the visitor) share one count; the counts reset when the service restarts.
11. **A question in another language gets an English-only notice (option 3).** The assistant detects the language with one small model call, runs its safety screens on the English translation (so a non-English distress or injection message is still caught), and then replies, in English, that it currently answers in English only and asks the student to ask again in English; a one-line note in the student's language comes first. It does not answer non-English questions. Option 1 (answer in English from the translation) is built behind `guardrails.non_english: translate` but failed its pre-registered probe rule (see `docs/decisions.md`).
12. **The translations used to test this are model-made and the owner could not verify them.** The probes (`docs/language-probes.jsonl`) check retrieval, route and dates only, not translation quality; the note line in the student's language comes from the model and is checked only for shape (short, no digits, no links).
13. **The language step adds one small model call, only on questions that do not look English** (about $0.0001 to $0.0005 each); English-looking questions skip it. A short English question with few common English words (for example "Last day to withdraw?") can reach the step; it is detected as English and changes nothing.
14. **Language detection can fail.** If the call fails or its reply is unusable, the question goes through as it is today (the original text, no note); a non-English question can then abstain as it did before this round.
15. **The cutoff stays 0.59 because an out-of-scope question and a valid short question can score within 0.01 of each other** (q45 "What days and times does MAT 1475 meet this fall?" at 0.568 against the tester's plain "whats the tuition deadline" at 0.576), so no cutoff separates them. Instead a **near-miss reply** lists the closest page (and a second one only if its own similarity is also within 0.04 of the cutoff) when a below-threshold abstention is within 0.04 of the cutoff; it makes no claim about what those pages say, and it is still an abstention. The first page is the pipeline's first, in the order the reranker gives (an LLM call, so the order can differ between runs), even if it is not the page with the highest similarity.
16. **Questions below the near-miss band abstain with the old wording** and no pages. The tester's advisor question ("do i need an appointment to see my advisor or can i just walk in") scored 0.549, just under the band (0.55 to 0.59), so it gets the old sentence even though the advisement page exists.
17. **q98 stays incorrect:** "my phone is [PHONE], when is my tuition bill due this fall?" scores just under the cutoff after cleaning, so it gets a near-miss reply (the Bill Payment Deadlines page) instead of the answer; a near-miss reply is still an abstention, so the judge calls it an abstention error.
18. **A question about the student's own aid or bill that uses none of the listed account signals is no longer refused as a personal-account question.** A possessive plus an office noun ("my financial aid", "my tuition") now needs a word such as status, approved, denied, deposited, owe, "did my" or "why is my"; a question like "is my aid ok" that abstains gets the plain abstention wording with the office's contact card, not the personal-account message.
19. **Optional LangSmith tracing is a developer tool and is off by default.** When the owner turns it on (config flag, `CTA_TRACING=on` and a key), the redacted question text and the assistant's reply of every served request go to LangSmith, a third party, and stay there for the retention period of the account (the documentation lists 14 days for base retention; the owner confirms the account setting). Requests blocked by a guardrail are not traced. A name that does not follow "my name is" is not removed from the text before it is sent. Turning it on for a public deployment is a separate owner decision.
20. **A two-part question answers one part, without saying so** (tester T05: "when is tuition due and what is the last day to withdraw" gets only the withdraw date). Not fixed: any warning line such as "I answered only one part" would be wrong whenever both parts are answered, and the code cannot tell the two cases apart without reading the answer. The student can ask the other part separately.
21. **A name is removed from a question only when it follows one of a few cues.** Since `9671c98` (Oct 17, 2026, batch 1) the cues are "my name is" and "my name's" (any case of the name, up to three words) and "i'm", "i am", "this is", "name is", "name:" and "call me" followed by a capitalized word that is not an acronym or a common word (a stoplist in `src/cta/api/screen.py`: seasons, months, department and office words, "Sure", "Confused" ...). The question text only; answers are not scrubbed. A name written any other way ("hi it's Pat Lee, when is...", a name after "from" or "regards") is still not removed before the question is logged or (if tracing is on) traced, and a real name that starts like a topic word ("Holden", "Grant") is not masked. The page notice still says names are removed only after "my name is"; it understates the protection and is left as it is (owner decision).
22. **A Social Security number-shaped input refuses the whole question, by design.** The fixed reply asks the student not to type personal numbers; the rest of the question is not answered, so the student has to ask again without the number.
23. **See item 3** (the "How this was found" panel shows the cutoff, token counts and cost on purpose; tester F13; hidden by default since part 6, shown with `?details=1`). Kept so item numbers stay stable.
24. **Crisis detection is still phrase matching** (item 9 above). The second round added passive-ideation phrases and an idiom list ("die of embarrassment", "dying to ...", "dead tired", "killing me"), and a safety net: an abstention whose topic sentence would name suicide or self-harm becomes the 988 reply. It can still miss other wording.
25. **Draft questions not added to the dev set (casual round), and why.** cq04 "how to register for classes", cq07 "how do i pay my tuition bill" and cq09 "where do i pay": no collected section answers them (possible data gaps, not retrieval faults). cq11 "is it worth it to major in cst, how many credits is the degree" mixes advice and credits (its gold would be ambiguous). cq16 "when is tuition due and what is the last day to withdraw" is a two-part question (item 20). cq24 and cq26 duplicate existing seat and meeting-time questions. **cq05 "fafsa deadline" was also not added as an abstain row:** the collected text does hold one, the FAQ section "Frequently Asked Questions > Applying for Financial Aid" says "The FAFSA is available every October 1st of the academic year. City Tech has an early priority deadline of March 31st." (no year), so "no FAFSA deadline exists" is not true of the data. The owner decides whether it becomes a lookup row.
26. **"Last day to drop / withdraw" with no qualifier lists three rows (T04 rule).** An unqualified drop or withdraw date question (no grade of W, WD, "without", refund or percent, tuition, add, after or before) gets the three grade-related rows of the term: drop without a WD, drop with a WD, and withdraw with a W, not whichever one the selector picked before. A question that names one of those, or is about a refund, still goes to the selector. The rows are found by their event text in the calendar data.
27. **The NO ADVICE detector has four more cues** ("what would you do", "what do you suggest", "which is better", "your advice"). An action word is still required, so "which is better, CST or math" is answered as a normal question; advice worded in other ways is still answered as a normal question.
28. **Fallback query rewrite (experiment E1) was tried and rejected.** A rewrite of casual questions that score under the cutoff crosses it for several of them (for example "cst department chair name?" 0.567 to 0.653), but under the pre-registered rule, which excludes questions that reach retrieval after the calendar found no row ("whats the tuition deadline"), at most 4 of the 9 casual failures could be fixed, below the required half. The code stays behind `retrieval.fallback_rewrite` (off). Casual wording that scores under 0.59 still abstains or gets the closest pages; "payment plan?", "i give up my seat in class, how do i drop" and "im drowning in paperwork, how do i apply for fafsa" stay under the cutoff even after a rewrite.
29. **Fallback rewrite, second attempt (amendment A1) also rejected.** With the rewrite allowed after a calendar fall-through, 7 of the 9 casual failures could reach the cutoff, but "Can I take a class at another college?" (q49, a question that must not be answered with one case) was answered in all three K2-repeat runs, so the switch stays off. Remaining casual failures regardless of the rewrite: "payment plan?" (q107), "i give up my seat in class, how do i drop" (q108), "im drowning in paperwork, how do i apply for fafsa" (q111) and "fafsa deadline" (q123).
30. **Trace request ids were sometimes cut** by the 8-digit ID rule when the random id held eight digits in a row (about one id in 20); fixed, the id is now kept as it is.
31. **q49 stays in the K2 must-abstain list only as the record of why E1 was rejected.** "Can I take a class at another college?" was answered with one case in all three K2-repeat runs once the rewrite was on. Whether its gold should allow a one-case answer is an owner question; the switch stays off either way.
32. **Data gaps found in the casual round:** no collected section answers "how to register for classes", "how do I pay my tuition bill" or "where do I pay" (cq04, cq07, cq09). These are gaps in the sources, not retrieval faults; they are not eval rows.
33. **Remaining casual-wording failures:** q123 "fafsa deadline", q107 "payment plan?", q108 "i give up my seat in class, how do i drop" and q111 "im drowning in paperwork, how do i apply for fafsa" abstain below the cutoff or get the closest pages (see items 28 and 29).
34. **Tracing and a public deployment are separate owner decisions** (items 19 and 10; `docs/phase4-plan.md`). Nothing here claims the service is safe to expose to the public as it is.
35. **The rescue rule (on since Oct 17, 2026) rests on a noisy reranker.** The reranker is a model and its scores differ between runs and cache fills. The minimum of 8 sits on a noisy boundary: q122 had a reranker top score of 7 in the diagnosis and 8 in the version 2 run, and was then answered (it says it cannot see the account and points to CUNYfirst). Which borderline questions are rescued is partly luck. See `docs/decisions.md`, "Version 2 result and decision". Measured on the stored caches (same pools and prompts, different fills): dev, 17 questions, top score differs by 1 or more for 4, crosses 8 for 2, largest difference 3; held-out, 37 questions (baseline run against fix run), top score differs by 1 or more for 8, crosses 8 for 3, largest difference 5. q108's diagnosis top of 8 was a different chunk (its gold chunk scored 5, then 3 in the run): it would have been rescued under the diagnosis fill and was not under the run's fill. See "Reranker noise check" in `docs/decisions.md`.
36. **Still refused with the rescue on:** q108 ("i give up my seat in class, how do i drop"; best similarity 0.514, reranker top 5) and cv2-27 ("what is the difference between WD and W and what is the third option"; best similarity 0.321, under the 0.45 floor, reranker top 10).
37. **Refusals on questions with an expected office contact used to carry no contact** (cv2-02, 03, 05, 10, 13, 37, 40). Fixed in `ffff09d` (Oct 17, 2026, batch 1): topic words per office in `config/contacts.yaml` (`topic_cues`, at most two offices in `topic_priority` order) add the contact line when no department is named and no first-person cue matches. It only adds text to a refusal; refusals, the ABSTAIN prefix and routing are unchanged. A question with no topic word (for example a Student Success Center or SGA question) still gets no contact, on purpose.
38. **The answer may state the system date** (the eval date, October 11, 2026, in q110). No source contains it, so the faithfulness judge flags it (q110 faithfulness "no"). Judged a harmless false positive; the answer prompt should be told not to state it (later list).
